We were handed maps by people
Who had never walked our roads,
Told that life would pay us back
For every burden, every load.
So we stitched our hopes to paper,
Wore ambition like a crown,
Never asking who we'd be
If the whole thing crumbled down.

We learned to count our future
In certificates and grades,
While childhood slipped behind us
Like the light through window shades.
Our mothers sold their little comforts,
Our fathers swallowed pride,
And we promised them a better world
We'd build when we arrived.

But the years brought folded letters,
Silent phones, and locked-up doors;
Degrees gathered dust beside us,
While we searched for something more.
We had done what we were told to do,
Yet somehow missed the prize,
With a thousand qualifications
And unanswered questions in our eyes.

We said, "Just wait until I'm settled,
Then I'll give them everything."
But the seasons took our parents
While we chased a distant spring.
We kept postponing Sunday visits,
Kept our apologies for later,
Never knowing time was writing
Its own name upon the paper.

We watched our friends become their titles,
Watched their laughter grow restrained,
Watched them trade their deepest longings
For a salary and a name.
Some bought houses full of silence,
Some wore gold to hide their scars,
Some became the very people
They once swore they'd never be or were.

**We were all sold the wrong dream,**
And we paid in more than gold;
We paid with years we can't recover,
With the stories left untold.
We were promised that the summit
Would make every wound seem small,
But nobody warned us climbing
Could mean losing it all.

We mistook a busy calendar
For a life that mattered more,
Kept on knocking at the future
While neglecting our own door.
We became so good at looking happy
That our sadness learned disguise;
Even those who knew our names
Could not read the weather in our eyes.

And what hurts most is not the struggle,
Nor the nights we went without;
It's the fear that we have built our lives
Around somebody else's doubt.
That we chased applause from strangers
While our own hearts begged for air,
That we became so hard to disappoint
We forgot to truly care.

Perhaps the dream was never evil;
Perhaps the lie was in its size.
They taught us how to reach for everything
But never taught us how to choose our lives.
They showed us what a winner looks like,
What a worthy life should mean,
But left us with no answer
When we questioned the machine.

So let me want a smaller morning,
Let me sit beside a friend,
Let me call my mother just to hear her
Without asking for the weekend's end.
Let me earn an honest living
Without selling every hour,
Let me have a little room to fail
Without believing I have lost my power.

I no longer need a crowded room
To convince me I belong;
I would rather have one honest hand
Than a thousand voices singing wrong.
Let the world mistake my quiet
For a life that fell behind;
I am done exchanging what is precious
For a place in somebody else's mind.

And when my hair has turned to winter,
When my hands have lost their speed,
May I find no list of vanished moments
That I sacrificed to greed.
May I remember faces, not achievements,
And the love I chose to keep;
May the life I leave behind be one
That lets my tired soul sleep.

**We were all sold the wrong dream,**
But the ending isn't sealed.
We can question what we worship,
We can change what we have healed.
We can lay those borrowed maps aside
And walk where our hearts lead,
For a life is not the one that looks successful—
It's the one that meets our deepest need.

And perhaps, when all is over,
When our names no longer gleam,
We'll discover we were never born
To be the proof of someone else's dream.
We were born to love while time remained,
To live before it passed us by—
Not to spend our whole lives chasing heaven
And reach it having forgotten why.
