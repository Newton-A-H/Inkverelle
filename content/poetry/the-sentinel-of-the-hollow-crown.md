In a hall where dying embers sleep,
He stands where shadows gather deep.
A crimson plume, a blade of night,
A nameless guard who serves the light.

No king commands, no trumpets call,
Yet still he answers to the hall.
His master’s words, now dust and air,
Are sacred vows he will not spare.

The world has moved, the years have fled,
The banners rot above the dead.
Yet every dusk, through frost and rain,
He takes his post and stands again.

Perhaps he knows his lord is gone,
That dawn will break, but not for one.
Still, should the heavens split apart,
He’d guard that throne with all his heart.

And if the stars should ask him why
He guards a crown beneath a dead sky,
He’d bow his head, his sword held high—
“I was his knight. That does not die.”
