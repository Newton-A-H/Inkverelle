There is a particular kind of quiet that arrives just before the day gets going. The kettle has not yet begun its small argument with the stove. The light is finding its way across the kitchen, one pale inch at a time.

I used to think attention was a talent, something given to people who knew how to be still. Now I think it is closer to a practice of return. You leave your thoughts a hundred times, and a hundred times you bring them back: to the lavender leaning over the fence, to the cup warming your hands, to the person who is telling you a story you have heard before.

The ordinary world is not waiting to become extraordinary. It is already here, asking only that we notice.
