Mara kept the tickets in a blue tin that had once held tea. She kept them folded and flat, each one a small map of a departure she had made.

There was the first train to the coast, where she slept through the station and woke with salt in her hair. There was the bus to the city, the window full of rain and a future she could not yet picture. And there was the return ticket, still crisp, from the winter she came home.

Her mother never asked why she saved them. Some things, Mara knew, were easier to carry when nobody tried to name them.
